\begin{abstract}
  Hierarchical task network (HTN) planning with nondeterministic actions asks for a way of
  refining an initial task network into executable actions that works no matter which outcome
  each action has. Two families of solution concepts have been proposed: \emph{fixed methods},
  where all refinement decisions are taken before execution, and \emph{method-based policies},
  where they may depend on the outcomes observed so far. Each family comes in a weak, a strong
  and (for policies) a strong cyclic flavour, and the complexity of deciding plan existence has
  been studied for the usual structural restrictions of the hierarchy. Several entries of the
  resulting tables were open, and several published proofs turn out to be incomplete or wrong.

  We close every remaining gap. For fixed methods, strong outcome-dependent solutions of acyclic partially ordered problems
  are \EXPSPACE-complete, and those of partially ordered tail-recursive problems are
  undecidable, as are those of regular problems whose initial task network is not regular. For method-based
  policies, totally ordered problems with an arbitrary hierarchy are \TWOEXP-complete for strong
  and for strong cyclic solutions. We argue that the published definition of strong cyclic
  solutions must be read with the usual fairness condition, and show that under this reading
  the unrestricted problem is not even semi-decidable: it is $\Sigma^1_1$-complete, and in
  particular outside the arithmetical hierarchy. Along the way we repair the published
  lower-bound proofs whose constructions fail, and we give new reductions where the published
  arguments are only outlines.

  All results except one classical theorem of recursion theory, which we cite, have been
  verified in the proof assistant Lean~4. This article presents the proofs in ordinary
  mathematical language.
\end{abstract}
